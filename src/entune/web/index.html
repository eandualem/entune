    How the dictionary works

    A short guide to fixing the words your speech model gets wrong.

    What it's for

    Every speech model has words it keeps getting wrong: names, products, technical terms. Your dictionary remembers those mistakes and fixes them in each new dictation, before the text reaches you.

    How an entry works

    An entry pairs what the speech model writes with what you might have meant. Say it often writes “cloud” when you said “Claude”. But sometimes you really mean a cloud. So the entry keeps both meanings:

    cloud → Claude, an AI assistant you ask to write or code
    cloud → cloud, internet storage and servers

    In “Ask cloud to draft the reply”, Entune writes Claude. In “The backups are in the cloud”, it leaves cloud alone. It chooses from the surrounding words, which is why each meaning has a short description: write what the thing is and what it goes with.

    One entry can list several spellings the model writes (“cloud”, “clawed”) and several meanings. Tick which meanings each spelling can stand for.

    Getting suggestions

    You don't have to build the dictionary by hand. Entune can read your dictations and suggest entries, using the dictionary model you choose (its key is in Settings).

    • Suggest new entries reads your recent transcripts and finds mistakes the dictionary doesn't cover yet. Start here.
    • Suggest improvements compares what the speech model wrote with what the dictionary changed, and suggests fixes: a missing entry, a clearer description, or removing an entry that does more harm than good.
    • From saved audio transcribes recordings again with your current speech model before suggesting entries. Use it when the recordings were made with another model, or come from Wispr Flow or a folder.

    Each speech model makes its own mistakes, so suggestions are kept for the speech model selected in the toolbar.

    Choosing audio

    After you pick a source, the timeline shows all its recorded audio, oldest on the left and newest on the right. It measures recorded time, so days without recordings take no space; the dates under the handles show where you are.

    Everything is selected at first. Drag the left handle right to keep only your most recent audio, or move either handle to choose any stretch. You'll see exactly how much audio and how many recordings are included, and you can list and play them. Transcribing again can take a while and your providers may charge for it.

    Reviewing suggestions

    Suggestions appear above your dictionary, grouped into new entries, changes and removals. You can edit any of them, remove the ones you don't want with ×, and then apply the rest in one step. Nothing is saved until you apply. While suggestions are open, dictation pauses, so apply or discard them when you're done.

    Pinned and learned entries

    Learned entries are used only with the speech model they were learned for. Pin an entry to use it with every speech model; pinned entries are yours and suggestions can't remove them. Pinning doesn't make an entry win: Entune still reads the sentence.

    Adding an entry yourself

    Choose Add an entry. Write what you meant and describe it, then add each spelling the speech model writes and tick the meanings it can stand for. You can collapse the form and come back to it, or discard it.

    Learn from audio

    Transcribe saved audio again with , then propose dictionary changes from the new text. History and your dictionary stay unchanged until you apply a proposal.

    The range follows recorded time, oldest to newest, without the gaps between recording days. Drag the left handle to keep the most recent audio; drag either handle for any continuous stretch. Each edge snaps to whole recordings.
